# evomi review: what $0.49/GB actually costs, where the 100 GB entry plan hurts, and when 9Proxy's pay-per-IP model is cheaper

Most people typing "evomi review" into a search bar are not looking for a company history. They want to know one thing: if the headline rate is $0.49 per gigabyte, why does the bill at the end of the month look nothing like that number?

That gap is real, and it is the single most useful thing to understand about Evomi before you deposit money. Below is the pricing structure as it is currently published, the limitations that show up in reviews and user complaints rather than on the marketing page, and where a completely different billing model — per IP instead of per gigabyte — turns out to cost less for the same job.

## The short version

Evomi is a Swiss proxy provider selling five product lines, and it is genuinely cheap at volume. Its Core Residential plan at $0.49/GB is one of the lowest published residential rates you can buy self-serve. But the cheapest plan that unlocks that rate is 100 GB per month for $49.99, which means anyone using 5–10 GB a month pays an effective rate far above the sticker.

If you scrape 50 GB or more monthly, or you want premium residential IPs for hard targets, Evomi is a reasonable buy and the free trial on Core Residential and Datacenter makes it easy to test before committing. If you run multi-account work with long sessions, need API access for provisioning, or burn less than ~50 GB a month, the math tilts toward a per-IP provider such as 9Proxy, where unused addresses don't expire and each IP comes with unlimited traffic.

## The 100 GB entry plan, and why the effective rate can be 20× the headline

Evomi's own product page states it plainly: Core Residential is $0.99/GB pay-as-you-go, or $0.49/GB with a monthly plan — and the smallest monthly plan is 100 GB.

An independent cost analysis published by API Serpent, working from prices verified 13 August 2026, put numbers on the consequence. At 5 GB of monthly usage, Evomi's effective rate is $10.00/GB, making it one of the two most expensive options in their comparison. At 50 GB, the same prices make it joint-cheapest at $1.00/GB.

Nothing about the pricing changes. The plan just doesn't shrink.

> If your smallest available plan is much larger than your actual volume, you pay for the whole plan. That is why a $0.49/GB rate becomes $10.00/GB for a 5 GB user.

So before you compare Evomi against anything else, work out your real monthly traffic. Under roughly 50 GB, you are buying 100 GB and wasting most of it — and that is exactly the scenario where a pay-per-IP or pay-small-package model wins on total spend.

## Where Evomi's review scores actually drop

The dashboard gets consistent praise. HostAdvice's hands-on review called it the most polished interface at this price point, and the per-product usage graphs (traffic volume and request count, filterable across today, 3, 7, 14, or 30 days) are enough for normal monitoring. On Trustpilot, Evomi sits at 4.3/5 with an "Excellent" rating and a steady run of reviews praising low fraud scores and low block rates.

The friction sits elsewhere, and it is worth knowing before you commit:

**API access is gated.** Evomi has a management API, but it requires a 5 TB minimum purchase and is limited to resellers. A standard account cannot programmatically provision or manage proxies. If your stack expects to spin endpoints up and down from code, this is a hard stop, not an inconvenience.

**You cannot choose your own credentials.** Usernames and passwords are generated by the system. For a solo operator that is fine. For a team where several people need independent access, credential management happens outside the platform.

**There is no sub-user system.** Combined with the login limitation above, this is the recurring theme in team-oriented criticism: everything is designed around one account holder.

**Sticky sessions can move.** This is the most common complaint in negative reviews — sessions that were supposed to hold for 30 minutes rotating repeatedly instead. Evomi's public response on Trustpilot is honest about the cause:

> Each IP in our pool comes from a real, active device, which can disconnect at any point in time. Because of this, we can't guarantee a fixed connection time for every session.

That is a structural property of real-device residential pools, not a bug unique to Evomi, and the vendor's suggested workaround is its static residential line. But if your workflow depends on holding one identity for hours — account management, social media, checkout flows — plan for replacement IPs and short session windows.

**Support starts with a bot.** Live chat opens with an AI assistant that answers technical questions accurately but does not hand you to a human until it extracts a one-line summary of the issue. Two escalation attempts in HostAdvice's test did not reach a person. Trustpilot shows the company replying to 55% of negative reviews, typically within 24 hours, which is a better record than most budget providers but not instant help.

## Who should still buy Evomi

The criticisms above are specific, not universal. Evomi holds up well if you fit one of these:

- You move 50 GB or more a month on rotating residential traffic and want the lowest per-GB rate that does not require a sales call.
- You need geo-targeting down to city level plus both rotating and sticky modes included in the base price.
- You want premium residential IPs for targets that block cheaper pools, at $2.20/GB rather than the $4+ that some competitors charge on their cheapest 100 GB plans.
- You need proxies that survive long-held connections and are willing to pay per IP for them — the static residential line at $1.00/IP exists for exactly this.
- You want to test before paying. Core Residential and Datacenter both run a free trial with a $0.00 checkout total (billing details are still required), and there is a money-back window if you have used less than 1 GB or 10% of the plan.

## 9Proxy approaches the same problem from the other direction

9Proxy bills per IP with unlimited bandwidth on that IP, rather than per gigabyte of traffic. Published rates start around $0.015/IP and $0.68/GB, with 20M+ residential IPs across 90+ countries and an advertised 99.95% uptime.

Two distinct systems run side by side, and the difference decides which one you want:

**Residential by IP.** You buy a package of addresses and each one carries unlimited traffic while active. Unused IPs never expire. Every IP stays alive for a few hours up to around 24 hours depending on the device behind it. Authentication runs through the 9Proxy desktop app with local port forwarding, and there is no natural rotation — you would add it through the Auto Rotation Proxy on selected ports. This model exists for session-persistent, account-based work where per-GB billing is a tax on a workload that barely moves data.

**Residential by GB.** You buy bandwidth and generate unlimited endpoints. Validity is 180 days on standard packages and unlimited on Enterprise. Sessions can be sticky or rotating, authentication is username/password or IP whitelisting, targeting reaches country, state, city, ZIP, and ISP levels, and everything runs from the dashboard with no app install. For automation and high-volume collection, this is the direct comparison against Evomi's Core Residential.

The notable structural point: because the by-GB side has no app requirement and supports sub-users with assignable traffic, the team and cloud-automation gaps that show up in an evomi review are handled at the account level rather than as a reseller-only feature.

## FAQ

**Is the $0.49/GB rate real?**
Yes, but only on the 100 GB monthly plan at $49.99 and above. Pay-as-you-go starts at $0.99/GB from 15 GB and falls to $0.79/GB from 1,000 GB. The rate is real; the plan size is the catch.

**Is Evomi trustworthy?**
It is a Swiss-registered provider with EWDCI certification claims and a 4.3/5 Trustpilot score across a mix of long-term users and a handful of sharp complaints about sticky sessions and support response time. The vendor replies publicly to most negative reviews, which is more than many budget providers do.

**Can I use Evomi teams or automate it?**
Not on a standard account. The API is reseller-only above a 5 TB threshold and there is no sub-user system, so multi-person access means sharing one login.

**Does 9Proxy require an app?**
The by-IP model does — it uses local port forwarding through the desktop client. The by-GB model runs entirely from the dashboard.

**Do unused 9Proxy IPs expire?**
No. By-IP packages stay usable until consumed, which is why the model suits bursty or batch workflows. By-GB traffic on standard packages carries a 180-day validity; Enterprise packages remove the expiry.

**Which one should you actually buy?**
If you push 50 GB or more monthly through rotating residential traffic and want the lowest rate without a sales call, Evomi's Core Residential plan is hard to beat, and the free trial removes the risk of finding out you were wrong. If you are under 50 GB a month, need one address to hold a session for hours, run several people or cloud instances off one account, or your traffic per IP is tiny, 9Proxy's per-IP and small-package pricing will usually come out ahead — and the invite-code discount applies from the first purchase.
